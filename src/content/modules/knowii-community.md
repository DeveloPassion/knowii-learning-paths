## What it is

Knowii is the hub. Courses, kits, workshops and tools are the spokes. If you only ever watch the videos and never show up in the community, you get maybe half of the value. The other half is asking a question when you're stuck and getting an answer from me or from someone who was stuck at the same place three months ago.

## Do this first

1. Fill in your profile. One sentence about what you do, one about what you want to fix. That's enough.
2. Post in the introductions space. Say what brought you here and what your notes look like today (scattered apps? paper? nothing?). This is the single best predictor of whether you'll still be here in six months. People who introduce themselves stay.
3. Bookmark the events calendar. Live sessions are where I answer questions in depth.

## Then

- Read the [Start Here](https://www.knowii.net/c/start-here) space. Short, and it explains how I want the place to work.
- Browse the events archive for your tier (see the events module). Recordings of every masterclass are there for Knowledge Builders and Masters.
- Open [concepts.dsebastien.net](https://concepts.dsebastien.net) and [notes.dsebastien.net](https://notes.dsebastien.net). Thousands of linked notes, the whole OSK user guide and all the theory, free. Don't try to read them. Use them like a dictionary when a course mentions something you don't know.
- Look at the current monthly challenge. Joining one gives you a deadline, and deadlines are how things get done.

## Check in, don't wait for notifications

Two honest caveats about Circle. Notifications don't reliably go out, and the weekly roundup email it sends is thin compared to what happens inside. If you rely on either, you'll miss most of it. Open the community once or twice a week on purpose. Start with [Announcements](https://www.knowii.net/c/announcements): that's where I post what's new (releases, events, changes to your products). Then the [feed](https://www.knowii.net/feed).

Worth knowing: I share the work I do in there very regularly. What I'm building, what I changed in my own system, drafts, experiments, the things that never make it to the newsletter. The community sees it first, and often only.

## Skip it if

You can't skip it. Even if you bought a standalone product, the community is where updates get announced and where you'll find the people using the same system as you.

## What it leads to

The rest of your path. Come back here every time you finish a module and post what changed for you. Writing it down is half the learning.
